      About / method

      The sun layers are map tiles computed for the whole urban area in one run: every 2 m cell within 150 m of a LINZ address in Wellington City, Porirua City, Lower Hutt City and Upper Hutt City ( addresses). Tiles exist for zoom 11–16 (≈2 m per pixel at zoom 16) and are enlarged beyond that. Click anywhere (zoom 14+) for the nearest address's garden and roof scores; address data loads in small chunks for the area in view.

      Sun and shade come from a horizon-angle model run on LINZ 1 m LiDAR surfaces (Wellington City 2025, Hutt City 2025, Upper Hutt City 2025, Porirua 2023, Kāpiti Coast 2024–25, gap-filled with the Wellington 2013–14 survey), plus an 8 m surface out to 12 km for distant hills. The sun's position is sampled every 10 minutes for all of 2026 (pvlib SPA). Cloud isn't modelled. Morning = sunrise to solar noon, Afternoon = solar noon to sunset. Garden = 1.7 m above ground at the LINZ address point; Roof = top of the matched LINZ building outline. Sea and areas without LiDAR are blank.

      Potential water view: from the LINZ address point, 1.5 m above the roof (or 4.5 m above the ground if the point isn't on a building; the highest spot within 5 m), 360 sight lines 1° apart are traced for 15 km over the same LiDAR surface (buildings and trees included, Earth curvature allowed for). A direction counts if it reaches the sea, the harbour or a lake or reservoir of at least 0.5 ha (LINZ coastline and lake outlines) above everything nearer. The width is the number of such 1° directions (a view needs 5° or more), the bearing is the middle of the widest continuous stretch, and the distance is to the nearest visible water. This is an upper bound: it doesn't know which way windows face, and it uses the surface as surveyed (2023–25, some 2013–14).
